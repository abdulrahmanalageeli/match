# Blind Match 5.0 — Participant-experience audit

**Audit date:** 10 September 2026  
**Primary route:** /event3  
**Primary implementation:** app/routes/event3.tsx  
**Decision:** no application, event-state, participant-data, or production-data changes were made.

**Product decision update:** Recommendations that would remove an encountered participant from the ranking, add a “not met” ranking partition, reinterpret an untouched order, or otherwise change matching inputs were declined. The implementation keeps the established complete-ranking and matching semantics.

## Audit basis and limits

This audit follows the real participant path from opening the link through final results. Evidence came from source inspection of the participant route, participant/admin APIs, matching and persistence utilities, styles, results route, privacy route, and relevant tests; read-only preview inspection at 390×844 and 320×568; and targeted reliability/security tests. The targeted selection passed 42/42, although several are static contract tests that confirm current behavior rather than judging its participant safety. This report does not claim repository-wide suite health.

The preview pass covered welcome, login, AI welcome success/failure, phase transition, journey/meeting, feedback, group reflection, break, and mobile-QA modes. It confirmed no horizontal clipping at the tested sizes, but did confirm the RTL slider-label reversal, below-fold feedback action on the shortest viewport, reversed RTL walkthrough fraction, and an enabled early-end action that visually resembles a disabled control.

No live participant records, production state, SMS delivery, or real event operations were changed or exercised. NVDA, TalkBack, VoiceOver, 200% system text, venue glare/noise, and live social interpretations still require device and event testing. Items that depend on those conditions are marked as likely risks; code/data-flow contradictions are marked confirmed.

## Evidence labels and scoring

- **Confirmed defect/design/copy/privacy/consent issue:** directly supported by source behavior or reproduced preview behavior.
- **Likely risk requiring browser testing:** implementation evidence exists, but the complete behavior needs device/browser verification.
- **Likely risk requiring live-event testing:** the interface creates a plausible interpretation that must be validated with participants and operations.
- **Intentional mechanic reviewed and excluded:** the behavior belongs to the accepted event format and is not itself a defect.
- Journey scores use 1 = poor/high effort and 5 = excellent/low effort. Emotional interpretations describe preventable readings created by the interface; they are not claims that every participant will react identically.


# A. Executive verdict

**Verdict: not ready for another live event until the Critical findings and the live-state High findings are corrected and rehearsed.** The intended format—assigned numbers and tables, timed rotations, private rankings, system allocation, suspense, and compatibility reveals—is not the problem. On the happy path, the interface usually gives a participant a strong table card, an understandable primary action, and a coherent sense of progression. The risk is that several non-happy paths communicate claims the implementation does not uphold or turn infrastructure failures into apparent social judgments.

| Dimension | Judgment | Evidence-based summary |
|---|---|---|
| Overall participant clarity | **Good on the happy path; weak during exceptions** | `JourneyCue` and `MeetingPass` establish “now/next” and make the table prominent (`app/routes/event3.tsx:724–835`). Clarity breaks when assignments change, a partner does not arrive, data is delayed, or a form is interrupted. Some recovery screens cannot distinguish “not assigned” from “query failed.” |

| Privacy and consent | **Blocked by confirmed contradictions** | The mood prompt says `سري · ما يطلع عليه أحد`, while the organizer API and admin UI expose the participant number/name, exact mood, and time (`event3.tsx:8804`; `api/admin/index.mjs:13743–13763`; `app/routes/admin3.tsx:8083–8093`). | Accessibility | **Partial, with blockers** | Reduced-motion support, touch targets, and several focused/live-region flows are good. | Readiness for a crowded live venue | **No** | A live system must keep the participant’s current table/partner authoritative, make assistance continuously reachable, preserve sensitive drafts, and label delayed/missing data as technical. Those properties are not currently reliable enough for a noisy, time-constrained venue. |

What must happen first:

1. Restore a persistent, discreet organizer-help entry point in every live phase and verify its delivered/pending/failed states.
2. Stop the two confirmed privacy/consent contradictions: named mood visibility and unsolicited sharing of inferred partner traits.
3. Replace URL-asserted test/admin access with server-verified authorization.
4. Make current phase, table, partner, and result readiness a single versioned server truth that survives auxiliary-service failures and reassignment.
5. Treat untouched rankings as unconfirmed, preserve feedback/contact drafts, add a “meeting did not happen” path, and distinguish technical delay from a negative social outcome.

The positive interaction patterns worth preserving are the large table presentation, explicit mutual-contact explanation before the choice, neutral “no” availability, overlapping-poll protection, server-clock correction, and reduced-motion support.

## B.1 Journey-wide interpretation

The experience is generally legible when data is fresh and assignments do not change: prominent table cards, phase labels, and large CTAs usually answer “where am I?” and “what now?”. Trust and agency drop precisely at the most sensitive moments—ranking, partner reveal, feedback/contact, help seeking, and final results—because the interface either asserts privacy it does not technically provide, lacks an explicit finality model, or translates backend ambiguity into a socially meaningful empty state.

## E.1 Cross-screen hierarchy rules

- Keep exactly one high-emphasis filled action per viewport. An explicit “no” or “decline contact” must remain equally easy to reach, but it should not be styled as failure.
- Keep the table number sticky and largest only while the participant is moving. After arrival, reduce it to a compact persistent chip so the question—not logistics—owns the first viewport.
- Keep organizer help persistent across every live phase. Its idle treatment is a neutral purple/cyan outline; state—not mere availability—changes its color.
- Place privacy, visibility, editability, and finality text before a sensitive action. Do not hide these consequences in post-submit confirmation or an expandable disclosure.
- On 320×568 and at 200% text zoom, the current instruction plus its CTA must fit without the participant needing to infer that the page scrolls. Where content cannot fit, use progressive disclosure and a sticky action bar.

## G.1 Canonical participant-visible state language

Use the same compact status vocabulary everywhere:

- `لم تبدأ` — neutral gray; no action recorded.
- `غير محفوظ` — neutral/amber outline; local changes exist.
- `محفوظ على هذا الجهاز` — neutral cyan; usable only as a recovery draft, never equal to submission.
- `جارٍ الحفظ…` — amber with spinner.
- `تم الحفظ` — green with check, only after server confirmation.
- `تعذّر الحفظ` — red with warning icon and adjacent `إعادة المحاولة`.
- `ستظل قابلة للتعديل حتى …` — explicit editable window.
- `تم الإرسال نهائياً` — final state only after a pre-submit consequence/review screen.
- `أُغلقت هذه الخطوة` — locked/expired, followed by the exact recovery path (`اطلب المساعدة` or `سيحاول النظام إرسال المسودة المحفوظة`).

# J. Accessibility findings

## J.1 Visual

- **Medium — low-contrast operational text.** Privacy/help text appears at 9–11px in gray-500/600 (`event3.tsx:2142–2150`, `2572–2575`; `QuestionSlideshow.tsx:315–364`). Raise meaningful support copy to at least 12px/gray-300 and normal instructions to 16px where possible. Test contrast on composited glass gradients, including outdoor glare.
- **High — subjective/error color collision.** The feedback slider maps a low feeling to red/warning and high feeling to green (`event3.tsx:5957–6048`). Use neutral selected styling plus verbal endpoints; reserve error/success colors for system state.
- **Medium — compatibility traffic-light interpretation.** Result colors can be read as bad/good person labels (`results.tsx:1129–1231`). Use a single informational scale and text explanation.
- **Medium — small-screen first-task displacement.** On 320×568, stacked meeting pass and journey guidance can put the actual question below the fold. Collapse confirmed logistics and preserve a sticky current action; verify at 200% zoom and with Arabic font scaling.

## J.2 Motor

- Preserve the generally adequate large primary CTAs and expand all icon-only close/back/support targets to at least 44×44 CSS px.
- The persistent support action and early-end control must remain reachable one-handed without a precision tap. The current absence of a support trigger is also an access-to-safety blocker (`event3.tsx:6201–6205`, `9742–9747`).
- Ranking drag must have non-drag alternatives: `رفع` / `خفض` buttons with accessible names, and keyboard arrow movement. Do not require fine motor drag-and-drop while standing.
- Sticky actions must respect safe-area insets and must not cover the last form control at 320×568 or when the software keyboard is open.

## J.3 Screen reader and keyboard

- **High — detailed results accordion inaccessible.** Replace clickable `<div onClick>` (`app/routes/results.tsx:1049–1055`) with a real `<button>` carrying `aria-expanded`, `aria-controls`, a visible focus ring, and a labelled controlled region.
- **Medium — exclusive choices use toggle semantics.** Login methods (`event3.tsx:2432–2462`), group reflection (`5462–5473`), and private ratings (`5857–5873`) use `aria-pressed`. Use native radios or `role="radiogroup"` with named `role="radio"`, `aria-checked`, roving `tabIndex`, and arrow-key behavior.
- **Medium — feedback/SOS modal containment.** The code has reusable `useModalFocus` (`event3.tsx:1048–1117`), but feedback only focuses the heading and SOS does not provide full inert/focus trapping (`5796–5799`, `6269–6279`, `6342–6357`). Apply the reusable trap, make background inert, lock body scroll, close on Escape where safe, and restore focus to the opener.
- **Medium — AI welcome focus loss.** A focused loading control unmounts when the result arrives (`8157–8160`, `8290–8307`). Keep a stable focus target or explicitly focus the completed heading. Announce completion once in `aria-live="polite"`.
- Announce timer thresholds, not every second: 5 minutes, 1 minute, 10 seconds, and expiry. Per-second announcements would make the product unusable.
- Reassignment, offline/stale, save failure, and server confirmation require concise polite live-region messages. A safety/support failure may use assertive announcement.

## J.4 Cognitive

- For ADHD, autistic, anxious, slow-reading, and low-confidence users, retain `JourneyCue`’s now/next pattern but reduce each live screen to one required action, one optional action, and persistent help.
- Explicitly mark `مطلوب`, `اختياري`, and `لا يلزمك إجراء الآن`; do not use completion meters for optional reflection.
- Break long privacy/analysis prose into a one-line consequence before the action and an expandable explanation after it.
- A table/partner correction must show both old and new values until acknowledged; silent replacement relies on memory and raises wrong-table risk.
- Do not turn system states into social judgments: use `التأخير تقني` and distinguish `قيد التجهيز`, `بيانات غير كافية`, and `تعذّر التحليل`.

## J.5 Motion

- Reduced-motion handling is a current strength: transition/reveal flows include reduced-motion/fallback patterns. Preserve equivalent content and timing independence.
- Routine polling or timer changes must not trigger large entrance animations. Pulse only for the final urgent timer band or a safety alert, and stop it after acknowledgement.
- AI typing and reveal animation must be skippable and must never delay access to table/partner information. Slow devices should render the final stable state without depending on an animation callback.

## J.6 RTL and Arabic

- **High data-entry defect:** the LTR range and RTL endpoints are visually reversed (`event3.tsx:6012–6048`). Isolate slider and endpoints in one LTR container, or use Arabic semantic choices instead of numeric endpoints.
- Walkthrough fractions need bidi isolation (`<bdi dir="ltr">1 / 3</bdi>`); current visual order is reversed in the mobile preview.
- Verify chevrons/back icons mirror with RTL while universally directional icons (play, phone, check) do not.
- Mixed Latin profile labels such as raw attachment/communication values should not be shown at all to partners. Any remaining English term requires a reviewed Arabic equivalent and bidi isolation.
- Test Arabic at 200% text size for line wrapping inside table chips, sticky bars, countdowns, and two-option consent cards.

## J.7 Sensory

- Timer/event sounds and vibration are triggered without an Event3-specific mute control (`event3.tsx:976–1000`, `9343–9345`). Provide `صوت واهتزاز`, `اهتزاز فقط`, and `صامت`; preserve text/visual alerts in every mode.
- Avoid bright red for five continuous minutes and avoid simultaneous animation, sound, vibration, and modal movement for a routine transition.
- A full-screen mood prompt during conversation can be overstimulating and socially exposing. Queue it for break/processing; allow `ليس الآن`.

## J.8 Live-event environmental accessibility

- Every route-relevant screen must surface table/partner/current action in a three-second scan under glare and noise; do not rely on sound, subtle gray, or organizer speech alone.
- Provide a discreet visual help request for shy, anxious, hard-of-hearing, or speech-disabled participants; include a predefined option such as `أحتاج مساعدة بعيداً عن الطاولة`.
- Wrong-table and absent-partner flows should be self-reportable without publicly naming or blaming the other participant.
- Test on 320×568 and 390×844, 200% text, screen reader, keyboard/switch input, reduced motion, sound off, poor Wi-Fi, and one-handed use while walking.

# L. Moments that matter

## L.1 Five strongest positive moments to preserve

1. **First table assignment:** `MeetingPass` makes the destination number highly scannable, which supports movement under venue distraction (`event3.tsx:786–835`). Preserve the dominant numeral until arrival is confirmed.
2. **Now/next orientation:** `JourneyCue` separates current and upcoming tasks (`event3.tsx:724–783`). Preserve this mental model while shortening live-screen prose.
3. **Contact-consent choice:** no default, mutual-only disclosure, and custom contact channel support meaningful agency (`event3.tsx:6088–6167`). Preserve equal yes/no reachability and mutual privacy enforcement.
4. **Resilient core client polling:** overlapping/out-of-order response prevention, visibility refresh, backoff, and clock-skew correction reduce many live-state hazards (`event3.tsx:459–599`, `9254–9263`). Preserve these invariants when decoupling optional heartbeat data.
5. **Reduced-motion and calm AI fallback:** participants can reach the same content without depending on animation, and failed personalization does not block entry. Preserve equivalence and non-personal error framing.

## L.2 Five most vulnerable moments

1. **Requesting help during an uncomfortable one-to-one:** the promise exists but the control is unreachable—the most serious immediate safety/agency gap.
2. **Partner reveal:** sensitive inferred traits may be exposed, and a late replacement can leave old partner/table information visible.
3. **Private feedback/contact submission:** rating valence is biased by color, the slider can be read backward in RTL, drafts are volatile, and first submission is effectively irreversible.
4. **Mood check beside another participant:** a full-screen interruption plus false secrecy language undermines honest disclosure and dignity.
5. **Delayed or incomplete final reveal:** an infrastructure failure can be rendered as a socially meaningful empty result.

## L.3 Five moments where visual hierarchy is most important

1. Moving to a newly assigned or corrected table: new table must overpower every prior value and decorative element.
2. Arrival confirmation: table/revision, confirmation state, and wrong-table/help route must be found in three seconds.
3. Active one-to-one: conversation should dominate; timer, help, and early end remain compact but unmistakable.
4. Feedback/contact: current question, privacy/finality consequence, and save status must outrank disclaimers and decoration.
5. Final reveal: actual outcome/contact state first, limited score second, optional AI prose last.

## L.4 Five moments where technical reliability is most important

1. Ranking confirmation before the matching engine consumes preferences.
2. Live table or partner reassignment and arrival reset.
3. Feedback/contact commit after a timeout, retry, refresh, or phase advance.
4. Core event-state heartbeat during weak venue connectivity.
5. Final result readiness, where “missing” must never masquerade as a rejection or low-quality result.

# Q. Final judgment

| Question | Plain answer |
|---|---|
| Would a participant usually know what to do? | **Usually, on the happy path.** The prominent table/partner cards, journey cue, and primary CTAs are effective. That answer becomes **no** when a table or partner changes, a form is below the fold, a meeting never occurs, or a request fails—the situations in which three-second clarity matters most. |
| Can private decisions remain private? | **Not reliably under the current claims.** Contact release itself has useful mutual server-side protections, but the named mood response contradicts `ما يطلع عليه أحد`, inferred attachment/communication traits are sent to peers, memory words can reach organizer exports despite `أنت فقط`, and URL credentials remain shareable. These are blockers, not copy polish. |
| Could technical failure be mistaken for rejection? | **Yes, repeatedly.** Ignored database errors, stale assignment state, raw “No Phase … match” messages, delayed/missing result fields, and readiness based on a dash can look like nobody chose the participant, the organizer forgot them, or their result was too poor to analyze. Every absence/failure state needs a stable code and an explicitly technical, non-social explanation. |
| Can a participant recover from common mistakes? | **Only partially.** Ranking draft synchronization and poll recovery are promising, but sensitive feedback/contact drafts do not survive the Event3 flow reliably, submitted choices lack a clear edit/finality model, multiple tabs are ambiguous, and no-show/accidental-close paths are weak. Recovery must restore the exact draft and show the canonical server-saved value. |
| Is the visual hierarchy suitable for a crowded live venue? | **Mostly in normal states, not in critical ones.** The table number and main CTA are strong, but organizer help is absent, the feedback CTA can sit below the fold with no scrollbar, the first conversation question can be delayed by stacked cards, early red timers create alarm, and enabled gray actions resemble disabled ones. |
| Is the final reveal emotionally responsible? | **Not consistently.** A dignified reveal is possible with the existing structure, but `مطابقة مثالية!`, traffic-light percentages, deterministic AI language, and `لم يتم تحليله` blur the line between limited signal, unavailable analysis, and human value. The reveal should retain excitement while explicitly limiting what scores mean and separating technical failure from neutral outcome. |
| What is the single most important improvement? | **Make discreet organizer support continuously reachable and operationally reliable in every live phase.** It is the only control that protects a participant when the product cannot predict whether confusion has become discomfort or a safety issue. Use a persistent labelled trigger with delivery/acknowledgment/failure states and an away-from-table option. |
| What must be fixed before the next live event? | **All four Critical items and the live-state High items:** reachable support; truthful/anonymized mood handling; removal or explicit consent for peer-visible inferred traits; server-verified test/admin access; versioned table/partner/arrival truth; auxiliary-independent core polling; explicit ranking intent; durable feedback/contact drafts and canonical confirmation; no-show branching; DB error discrimination; RTL slider correction; and removal of bearer tokens from navigable URLs. Rehearse table reassignment, partner replacement, offline recovery, support delivery, and missing-result recovery with organizers on real phones. |
| What can safely wait? | **Polish that does not compromise truth or recovery:** ranking podium restyling, walkthrough fraction direction, compacting card nesting, coordinator terminology, AI-welcome focus refinement, optional-feedback tone, and sensory preference refinements can follow the release blockers—although the low-effort accessibility/copy items should be bundled into the same pass where practical. |

**Bottom line:** Blind Match 5.0 has a credible mobile happy path and several unusually thoughtful building blocks, especially its table presentation, mutual-contact explanation, polling safeguards, and reduced-motion support. It is not yet safe to treat that happy path as event readiness. The next release gate should be whether the participant receives one truthful, versioned answer to five questions at every moment: where am I, what do I do, what happens next, was my private action confirmed, and how do I get discreet help? Until privacy claims, help access, and failure-state semantics satisfy that gate, the event should not run live.
