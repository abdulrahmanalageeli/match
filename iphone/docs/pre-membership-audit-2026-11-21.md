# Wudayn pre-membership audit — 21 November 2026

## Decision and evidence limits

**Release readiness is unproven; do not use this review as approval to welcome paying users.** The strongest issue found is a mismatch between the promised control over sensitive publication and the new-registration gate. The second major issue is the size and uncertainty of the mandatory journey, which needs real-device and participant testing before a conversion claim can be made. This is a partial audit, not the exhaustive end-to-end audit requested.

Evidence labels: **Observed** = rendered browser state personally inspected; **Verified in isolated fixture** = automated test with mocked services; **Code finding** = present in source, not exercised end to end; **Hypothesis** = plausible risk needing research; **Opinion** = design judgment. Severity reflects potential impact, not a measured abandonment rate.

The host is Windows; an iOS simulator was unavailable. Tests used isolated Chrome mobile emulation, not native WebKit, a real account, live AI, payment, or production data. The browser fixtures replaced services; the account fixture stubs SMS and the assessment fixture mocks interview responses. Initial sandboxed localhost runs were denied; approved local execution subsequently worked. A legacy full-signup fixture failed to connect in the sandbox and was not completed. Screens outside the evidence index below were not visually inspected in this run. No complete male or female journey, purchase, approval, rejection or real transition to main app was exercised. No conclusions about native keyboard, safe areas, motion, voiceover, real-world speed, or live reliability are justified. The 375, 390, 402, 430 and 440 viewport matrix is incomplete: only the account fixture at 390 × 844 and hybrid fixture at 402 × 874 were run. Several screenshot filenames contain “390” despite the latter fixture's 402 viewport.

## C. Friction and trust

The current design collects considerable structured information before approval. Evidence of burden is the required instrument and profile validations, not measured abandonment. Family timing and next-step topics recur in interview, preferences and readiness; the readiness check has a legitimate conflict-resolution purpose, but whether repeat wording feels redundant needs participant testing. Profile self-description repeats the free-text introduction in a different privacy context; carry-forward should remain explicitly editable and separately consented. The mandatory model introduction followed by many direct answers may feel slow if few supported suggestions arise. The fixture measures 42/41/40 control actions for zero/one/three supported suggestions under its own counting rules; this is not human time, not the full registration, and is not directly comparable to older recorded fixture numbers without checking changed scenarios.

The source gates registration on both sensitive processing and publication (`GuidedApplication.tsx:549`), while consent detail says publication can be disabled while retaining private processing (`ConsentChoices.tsx:9`). **High severity, confirmed code-level mismatch**: a user who declines publication cannot proceed through the normal full-registration details step, despite that promise. The required UI strips “optional” from titles in this context; nevertheless the explanatory promise and gate conflict. Review with product/privacy counsel; don't assume legal conclusions. Test both gender flows and retention after changing consent, and decide whether publication is truly necessary before specifying a fix.

## D. Luna architecture and assessment

`interview.mjs` starts with the opening prompt, optionally asks a grounded next-step follow-up, persists transcript and supports retries; skips do not trigger an extra model request. `interview-grounding.mjs` checks literal evidence and independently verifies eligible ambiguous catalog mappings; UI accepts only `carryForwardIds`, with manual answers taking precedence. A suggestion is not a confirmed answer; self and desired-partner answers remain distinct. Final structured receipt and manual review preserve correction. The required direct selection interface is more reliable than repeated generative turns for fixed options; retain conversation for a short introduction, clarification and reflection where it adds information. Avoid adding AI calls merely to make the experience feel conversational.

Evidence: 130 targeted interview/API/UI tests passed. The isolated current browser fixture passed explicit partner choice, saved draft, 47-field confirmation, conditional parenting, historical 44-answer resume, opening example and optional next-step paths. It uses simulated model responses and cannot establish model accuracy, realistic language understanding, provider latency, or production persistence. Documentation in `guided-interview.md` describes an older six-theme/12-personality design; it is not sufficient to describe the current onboarding implementation and should be reconciled with current versioned behavior.

## E. Design and motion assessment

The inspected entry and Luna screens have a restrained palette, readable heading hierarchy and culturally appropriate Arabic voice; they are stronger than a generic chatbot. Repeated thin-bordered white rounded groups and burgundy microcopy risk blending controls into a single visual rhythm. This is a limited design opinion, not a full-system audit. Make information status, editorial content and interactive controls more distinguishable before introducing decorative metaphors. A compact “personal profile taking shape” motif could show meaningful completed domains, but prototype only if users understand it faster than the existing progress indicator. Keep motion short and functional; compare native transitions and reduced-motion behavior on device before specifying changes. No motion was directly inspected here.

## F. Reliability and safety findings

- **High / code finding:** sensitive publication gate conflicts with copy (section C).
- **Medium / source finding:** `saveApplication` saves the profile before photo finalization; its error explicitly says profile saved but photo failed (`App.tsx:585`). This is an intentional partial-success state, not a proven data-loss bug. Verify that retry cannot duplicate submission and that the next screen clearly explains the saved profile and unsaved photo.
- **Medium / hypothesis:** service-unavailable fallback depends on an explicit failure state; verify recovery when provider hangs, device goes offline or session expires. No live failure was exercised.
- **Medium / documentation finding:** older guided-interview description disagrees with current assessment contract and current source; this could mislead QA or release decisions. Version/retire it after confirming intended use.
- Unknown: failed uploads and permission denial; duplicate account, email/SMS delivery, native purchase cancellation/failure, rejection and resubmission, offline and expired-session recovery, interruption for days, double submissions in full flow, actual main-app transition. These remain release checks, not passing results.

### Refined findings

1. **Sensitive publication — privacy/product decision still unresolved.** The current registration slide explicitly states that both permissions are required, and labels the required choices accordingly (`SignupBasicsSlides.tsx`). That is an important qualification to section C: the user is told of the requirement on this screen. The broader consent description also says that publication can be turned off while private processing continues, and the final settings route advertises control. This may describe a post-registration ability rather than an outright contradictory statement. What is confirmed is that *new full-registration applicants cannot continue without accepting publication*, despite separate processing/publication controls. Verify actual post-registration withdrawal and consult the product/privacy owner on necessity and wording. This audit cannot resolve policy or legal compliance, and did not change it.
2. **Required skin tone — observed female fixture.** The appearance slide blocked progress until a skin tone was selected, while clothing had a prefer-not-to-disclose selection. This is consistent with source validation and the screenshot. The necessity and impact of requiring a sensitive appearance detail before application are unproven; review the purpose, disclosure and alternatives with privacy/product specialists and representative users. No legal conclusion is implied.
3. **Profile draft authenticity — code observation and UX hypothesis.** The fixture confirms profile text can be generated and rephrased without a model call. `profileDraftFallback.ts` also supplies generic first-person statements about values and desired relationship when no relevant selections exist. Although editable and shown for review, these statements may be accepted without reflecting the applicant. Test whether users recognize generated assumptions and edit them; favor neutral placeholders or clearly marked suggestions if participant research confirms the risk. Do not represent synthetic text as extracted facts.
4. **Payment state — observed fixture, not transaction validation.** The waiting screen clearly distinguishes approval from availability and says no purchase is required yet; the rendered disabled purchase button and price card may still look like an invitation to buy, a design hypothesis for usability testing. Verify all live introductory offer, renewal and localized price details in StoreKit sandbox. `App.tsx:637` refreshes eligibility and gender, requests a signed transaction, posts it for verification, completes it, then navigates to main. That is a source trace, not a successful real transaction.
5. **Rejection scope — source finding.** `AccountState.user.applicationStatus` declares `draft`, `pending`, and `approved` (`src/types.ts:66`); the main app branch sends non-approved applications to `Pending`. This does not prove the backend never records rejection or handles resubmission elsewhere. Trace the actual administrator/backend workflow and test a safe account before claiming a rejection journey exists or is missing.
6. **Recovery and fallback remain unverified end to end.** The legacy browser fixtures did not reach their assertions. Although prior tests cover mocked interview draft/answer handling, app restart, expired session, direct-question fallback during a current real browser journey, and partial photo save/retry need fresh fixtures or safe test accounts.
